How the simulation works

Index returns

Random markets draw each day's index return from a normal distribution, or a fat-tailed Student-t distribution with 4 degrees of freedom scaled to the same volatility. Daily volatility is annual volatility ÷ √252 and daily trend is annual trend ÷ 252. The seed makes every run repeatable.

Fund returns

Each day the fund earns its multiple of the index return plus a carry term for fees and financing:

fund return = k × index return + [(1 − k) × SOFR − swap notional × spread − expense ratio] ÷ 252

Swap notional is k − 1 for long funds and |k| for inverse funds. Long funds pay to finance their extra exposure. Inverse funds earn interest on cash collateral. If a single day's loss would exceed 100%, the fund goes to zero and stays there.

Why the gap appears

With no costs, a fund held for n days ends close to:

(1 + index return)k × e−½ k(k − 1) σ² n − 1

σ² is the variance of daily index returns. For any long multiple above 1× and for every inverse fund, k(k − 1) is positive, so volatility pulls the result down. In a steady trend with little volatility, the first term can push the fund above k × the index return.

A two-day example

The index rises 10%, then falls 10%: $100 to $110 to $99, a 1% loss. A 2× fund goes $100 to $120 to $96, a 4% loss. Twice the index's return would have been a 2% loss.

Why we show medians

Averaged across many markets, a fund with no costs lands close to k × the index return, because a few big winners offset many shortfalls. The median shows what a typical holder would have seen.

Limitations

  • Volatility, trend, SOFR and swap spreads are held constant. Real markets change.
  • No tracking error, trading costs, bid-ask spreads, premiums or discounts to NAV, or taxes.
  • The index is hypothetical. Results are not the performance of any fund or index.
  • Real funds may use different instruments and financing terms than modeled here.
